# ATHAR Research Library — Verified Starter Dossier
**Date:** 2026-10-27  
**Purpose:** Research handoff for an original Arabic interactive mystery-learning platform for audiences around ages 16–22, with age-appropriate variants later.  
**Status:** Starter dossier, not a comprehensive corpus. The URLs were located in current search results during this session; source depth and content licenses must be assessed individually before incorporation. No full copyrighted books have been downloaded.

## Executive concept
ATHAR is an original interactive case universe combining excellent fair-play mystery writing, evidence-based reasoning, introductory forensic science literacy, investigative ethics, and valid assessment of thinking skills. Users enter to solve mysteries; they leave with better-calibrated reasoning. Case facts must be authored, constrained, and reviewed; generative models may present conversations and feedback but must not fabricate evidence or determine the scientific truth dynamically.

## Catalog conventions
- **US-PD-claimed** = Project Gutenberg marks this *edition* as public domain in the United States, not necessarily in the country of deployment, and not necessarily translations/illustrations/branding.
- **Read-only / license TBD** = online resource verified; do not assume reproduction, redistribution, or commercial model-training permission.
- **Official guideline** = reliable authoritative context; distinguish a standard, draft standard, article and scholarly hypothesis.

## Design principles derived for examination (hypotheses, not validated ATHAR findings)
1. Author a stable case truth before any AI dialogue.
2. Give every character separate knowledge, beliefs and motives.
3. Distinguish observed data, hearsay, interpretation and inference.
4. Always give the learner a fair opportunity to resolve a case without undisclosed crucial facts.
5. Present plausible alternate hypotheses, not only a single line of deduction.
6. Encourage disconfirming evidence rather than only confirmation.
7. Require an explicit reason for conclusions; random correct guesses do not imply mastery.
8. Make uncertainties visible and reward 'insufficient evidence' where appropriate.
9. Keep science simulations appropriate to the fictional stakes and scientifically reviewed.
10. Do not portray stress, eye movement or body language as conclusive lie detection.
11. Each case teaches no more than a few named skills, with measurable indicators.
12. Give feedback on reasoning quality without revealing the answer too early.
13. Test independent transfer: skill performance on previously unseen cases.
14. Track explainability of assessments and bias between writing styles/dialects.
15. Protect minors and make nonviolent mysteries the default.
16. Use original characters and stories; copyright clearance before commercial reuse.
17. Keep dramatic stakes high without relying on gore or real-world victimization.
18. Compare competitors' mechanics and substitutes honestly; avoid claims of 'no competitors'.
19. Control marginal AI costs per case using bounded states and deterministic rules where possible.
20. Start with one excellent 25–40-minute original case and expert review, not a huge untested catalog.

## First case blueprint suggested for validation
**Title:** The Last Observation / الرصد الأخير.  
**Premise:** A supposedly unreleased astronomical observation appears in an anonymous message; a telescope image, access log and witness narrative disagree.  
**Learning goals:** evidence reliability, independent corroboration, timestamps, confidence and alternative explanations.  
**Mechanics:** examine staged observations; ask fictional witnesses grounded questions; visualize temporal discrepancies; run a safe synthetic-image/timestamp comparison; submit a justified conclusion or declare insufficient evidence.  
**Scoring evidence:** correct separation of fact and assumption, rejected unsupported claims, valid alternatives, defensible final report.
