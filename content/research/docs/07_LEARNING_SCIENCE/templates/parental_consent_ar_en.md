نحن نطوّر لعبة تعليمية بالعربية. يحل فيها اللاعب ألغازاً بجمع الأدلة والتفكير فيها، ولا عنف فيها.

### 2. ماذا سيفعل ابنكم أو ابنتكم؟
- يلعب [●] قصة أو قصتين قصيرتين، على الورق أو على هاتف أو حاسوب نوفره نحن.
- يقول ما يفكر فيه بصوت عالٍ وهو يلعب، ثم يجيب عن أسئلة قصيرة عن رأيه.

- [للتجربة 2 فقط] سيلعب ابنكم إحدى **نسختين كاملتين** من اللعبة، تختلفان في شاشة واحدة. تُختار النسخة بالقرعة. هدفنا أن نعرف أيهما أفضل، **ويمكنه تجربة النسخة الأخرى بعد انتهاء الدراسة.**

### 3. من سيكون حاضراً؟
- يحضر الجلسة **راشدان دائماً**، ولا يبقى ابنكم وحده مع شخص واحد.
- في الجلسة عن بعد، نطلب أن يكون أحد الوالدين في البيت أثناء الجلسة.

### 4. هل المشاركة إلزامية؟
- **لا.** المشاركة اختيارية تماماً.
- عدم المشاركة لا يؤثر في درجات ابنكم ولا في علاقته بمدرسته.
- نسأل ابنكم أيضاً إن كان يريد المشاركة. **إذا قال «لا» فلن يشارك، حتى لو وافقتم أنتم.**
- يمكنكم أو يمكن ابنكم التوقف في أي وقت دون ذكر السبب، ودون أي أثر سلبي.

### 5. هل فيها خطر؟
- الخطر المتوقع لا يزيد على نشاط صفي عادي.
- قد يشعر ابنكم بشيء من الملل أو الإحباط إذا صعب اللغز. وفي هذه الحالة نعطيه تلميحات، أو نتوقف.
- القصص لا تحتوي عنفاً أو مشاهد مخيفة، وقد راجعها مختصون قبل الدراسة.
- **لن نسأل** عن الأسرة أو الصحة أو الدين أو السياسة.

### 6. ما الفائدة؟
- لن يحصل ابنكم على فائدة مباشرة مؤكدة، لكنه سيتمرن على التفكير في الأدلة.
- ستساعد ملاحظاته في جعل اللعبة أفضل لغيره. وسنرسل لكم بعد الدراسة رسالة قصيرة عمّا غيّرناه بسبب ملاحظات المشاركين.

## English

**Study title:** [●]
**Responsible institution:** [university/research body] — Principal investigator: [●]

### 1. Why are we contacting you?
We are developing an Arabic learning game. Players solve mysteries by collecting evidence and thinking about it, and there is no violence in it.
We want to know whether teenagers find it fun and easy to understand. That is why we are inviting your son or daughter (aged 13–16) to take part.

### 2. What will your child do?
- Play [●] short stories, on paper or on a phone or computer that we provide.
- Say out loud what they are thinking while they play, then answer short questions about their opinion.
- **Length:** about [●] minutes, with a break.
- **Place:** [school / university office / remotely by video call with your child's camera off].
- [Experiment 2 only] Your child will play one of **two complete versions** of the game, which differ in one screen. The version is chosen by lottery. We want to find out which version works better, **and your child can try the other version after the study ends.**

### 3. Who will be present?
- **Two adults are always present**, so your child is never alone with one adult.
- For remote sessions, we ask that a parent be at home during the session.

### 4. Is taking part required?
- **No.** Taking part is completely voluntary.
- Saying no does not affect your child's grades or their relationship with the school.
- We also ask your child whether they want to take part. **If your child says "no", they will not take part, even if you have agreed.**
- You or your child can stop at any time without giving a reason, and without any negative effect.

### 5. Are there risks?
- The expected risk is no greater than an ordinary classroom activity.
- Your child may feel some boredom or frustration if a puzzle is hard. If that happens, we give hints or stop.
- The stories contain no violence or frightening scenes, and specialists reviewed them before the study.
- **We will not ask** about family, health, religion or politics.

### 6. What are the benefits?
- There is no guaranteed direct benefit, but your child will practise thinking about evidence.
- Their feedback will help make the game better for others. After the study, we will send you a short note about what we changed because of participants' feedback.

### 7. Thank-you
- Your child receives [a small gift / book voucher worth ●], **whether or not they finish the session.**
- We reimburse your travel costs: [●].

### 8. What data do we collect, and how do we protect it?
**What we collect:**
- Age band (13–14 or 15–16), gender, city, home language or dialect, and gaming experience.
- What your child does in the game and what they say about it.

**What we do not collect:**
- **We do not film your child's face or take photographs.**
- We do not ask for your child's phone number or social-media accounts.

**How we protect it:**
- We replace your child's name with a code, such as P-014. The list linking names to codes is kept separately, locked, and held by only two people.
- We store data in [country/institution] on encrypted devices. **We do not send your child's words to any AI service.**

**How long we keep it:**
- Audio recordings are deleted after they are typed up, and within [30] days at most.
- The list of names is destroyed [6] months after the final report.
- Data that does not identify anyone is kept until [●].

**How we publish results:**
- Results may appear in a report or scientific paper, **without any name or detail that could identify your child or their school.**

### 9. Limits of confidentiality
- We keep what your child says confidential.
- **However**, if your child says something showing that they or someone else is at risk of harm, we may inform [the study safeguarding lead / the competent authority, as required by law and school policy] so that help can be given.
- We will tell you and your child about this whenever it is safe to do so.

### 10. Withdrawing later

- After that date, the data no longer carries a name and cannot be separated out.
